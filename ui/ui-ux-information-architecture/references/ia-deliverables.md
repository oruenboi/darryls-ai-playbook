# IA Deliverables

Use this reference to structure UX and IA outputs.

## Deliverables as needed
- Journey map with entry points, goals, and drop-off risk.
- Task flow with interruptions, resumable steps, and edge paths.
- Navigation map with primary, secondary, and contextual layers.
- Content-priority matrix for each key screen.

## Quality Checks
- Keep labels user-centered and action-oriented.
- Minimize depth for frequent tasks.
- Ensure first-time and power-user paths both exist.
- Make progressive disclosure reversible and predictable.

## Task walkthrough

Follow a representative user's entry, decision, action, feedback, and recovery. State what the person needs to know at each step and what context they would otherwise have to remember. Use novices or frequent users when relevant; do not invent measured abandonment or research results.

For operational screens, preserve familiar navigation, necessary density, and expert shortcuts. For marketing surfaces, check that the offer, evidence, and next action are understandable without relying on decorative slogans. A reading page needs findability and readable structure; it need not behave like a sales funnel.

Distinguish an empty account, a filter with no matches, a failed load, and insufficient permission. Give each an accurate explanation and appropriate next step. Do not hide core controls behind forced onboarding. Preserve edits and selection context across interruption where the workflow requires it.

## UI copy

Name actions and consequences, especially destructive or irreversible ones. Explain errors near the affected control, including how to recover. Keep established domain terms, amounts, units, and qualifications. Do not invent claims or examples to make a page more persuasive. Use a full prose-editing skill only when the request warrants it.

Recommend grouping based on the actual decision burden. Do not apply a universal four-option limit or remove useful navigation simply to reduce visible counts.
